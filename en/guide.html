Online CNC guide: design, V-carving, 3D carving and PCB

Router Studio is free CNC software that designs shapes and text, V-carves and makes inlays, carves 3D models and photos, cuts DXF/SVG drawings, mills PCBs from Gerber files, simulates the job in 3D and streams it to your GRBL machine from the browser. No install; your files never leave your computer.

The interface at a glance

  • Top menus. File: new project, open, save, add file, Gerber folder. View: layer panel, rulers, grid, rapid moves, zoom. Help: guide, assistant and version information (About).
  • Left toolbar. Shapes, text, freehand drawing, nesting, apps and file import.
  • Canvas. Millimetre rulers along the top and left edges. Drag a design to move it; right-click to duplicate, align or delete. Switch between Design, Preview and 3D.
  • Right panel. The strip at the top holds material, work zero and bit settings. The selected design has two tabs: Cut (cut type, bit, depth) and Shape and position (size, angle, text, position).
  • Machine tab. Three steps: 1 Connect, 2 Set the zero point, 3 Carve.

Designing shapes and text

  1. Add. Pick a shape or text in the left toolbar. The new design lands in free space on the stock and the Shape and position tab opens.
  2. Size it. Enter width, height and angle; "Keep proportions" scales evenly. Set the corner radius, number of corners, font and letter height.
  3. Edit. "Advanced vector editing" offers offset copies, simplify, point editing and union, subtract or intersect with a second shape. The freehand tool draws by hand.
  4. Arrange. Use the align buttons, "Center on material", the right-click menu and Nest in the toolbar. Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo.

Overlapping parts of letters are merged into one outline, so every cut follows only the visible edge. If the bit does not fit a thin part of a letter, the app tells you how much of the area will stay uncut.

V-carving and inlays: step by step

  1. Select the shape and set the cut type to V-carve in the Cut tab. A suitable V-bit is assigned.
  2. Enter the depth. This is the maximum depth; wider areas get a flat floor. Pick a "Flat-floor bit" to clear them quickly.
  3. For an inlay press "Add V-inlay plug". A mirrored plug is created; the "Start depth" leaves a glue gap between plug and pocket.
  4. Cut separately. Cut the pocket in one board and the plug in a second board of another colour; hide the one you are not cutting. Glue the plug in and sand flush. Try it on scrap first.

3D carving: step by step

  1. Add the model. Use File → Add file to choose an STL, OBJ, 3MF or a photo. The model shows in 3D; orbit it with the mouse.
  2. Set size and orientation. Choose the width, depth and the face that points up. Tick "Invert" for a mould.
  3. Choose the bits. An end mill (e.g. 3.175 mm) for roughing and a ball nose for finishing. Set the raster to "X + Y" for a smoother surface.
  4. Simulate and carve. Check the carved surface in 3D. Optionally cut the part free along its outline.

PCB from Gerber: step by step

  1. Open the folder. Use File → Open Gerber folder. Copper, outline and drill layers are recognised; mask and silkscreen are skipped.
  2. Enter the material. In the Material item of the strip, type the size of the copper board and place the PCB on it.
  3. Choose the bits. A V-bit for isolation (e.g. 30° · 0.2 mm), a 0.8 mm end mill for holes and a 1 mm end mill for the outline. The app works out the V-bit groove width from the depth.
  4. Simulate and carve. Jobs run by bit: isolation, drilling, outline. After each job the machine parks and guides you through the bit change.

Nesting, fill and apps

  • Nest. Arranges every part on the stock, largest first; parts that do not fit are left to the right of the stock.
  • Fill the stock with this. From the right-click menu, fills the stock with copies of the selected part around the other parts.
  • Apps. Box maker (5 finger-jointed panels), gear, maze, honeycomb, hole grid, radial array and dog-bone corners. Dog-bones add bit-radius reliefs to inner corners so joints fit fully.

Carving: three steps at the machine

  1. Connect. Press Connect in the Machine tab and pick your GRBL board's port. Set the work area and the park position in the machine profile.
  2. Set the zero point. Jog the bit to the start point, set the XY zero, then the Z zero with the paper method. "Trace frame" checks the position without cutting.
  3. Carve. Start the jobs in order; a checklist opens first. While cutting, change feed and spindle speed in 10% steps. If a bit breaks or the job stops, use "Resume from line".

For stock longer than your machine, turn on tiling in the Work zero panel: the job is split into bands along Y and you move the stock by the given length after each band.

Connecting a machine

Router Studio works with machines running GRBL 1.1: Arduino Uno + CNC Shield V3, 3018-style desktop routers and similar. It connects through the browser's Web Serial API, so use Chrome or Edge. You can also download the G-code as an .nc file and send it with Candle or UGS.

Frequently asked questions

How do I carve text or a logo?

Add Text from the left toolbar or open your SVG/DXF logo. Use V-carve for sharp corners, Pocket for flat-bottomed lettering or Outside to cut the letters out as parts.

Can I use it without a Z probe?

Yes. Lower the bit onto a sheet of paper and press "Set Z zero here". With a probe you can also use automatic Z probing and a height map (auto-level).

Can I open DWG files?

DWG is a closed format. Save the drawing as DXF in AutoCAD, LibreCAD or ODA File Converter and import that.

Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. Everything runs in your browser; your files never leave your computer.

How is it different from Easel?

Router Studio is free with no carve limit, and V-carving, V-inlays, 3D carving, textured pockets, nesting and tiling are open to everyone. It also reads Gerber and Excellon files directly, mills PCB isolation paths and offers height maps (auto-level).
